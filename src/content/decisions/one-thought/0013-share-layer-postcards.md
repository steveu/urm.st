The growth patterns behind today's fastest-growing consumer apps — Barnum-effect personalisation, onboarding that makes you invest effort, identity labels, sharing as a growth loop — are strong pulls. They seem to collide head-on with this product's thesis: thoughts *graduate*, your use of any one thought falls to zero, and the app coaches you not to need it. (The source I was studying was Tim Gabe's *The Twisted Psychology Behind Top 1% Apps*.)

The reconciliation: the collision only happens at the level of mechanism. At the level of psychology, graduation is the strongest label there is — a **mastery identity** ("I outgrew the thought") rather than a dependency identity (a streak). Identity attaches to the golfer and the method, not to how often you open the app, so it survives the fade to zero. The retention thesis becomes *the book is where my golf lives*; the acquisition thesis becomes *postcards from it*.

The decision: One Thought gets a share layer, governed by mastery identity and built as **postcards**.

- **Round postcards first; graduation cards are the destination.** The graduation card may ultimately be the product, but the concept is early, and round cards gather the data and the use. Both speak one visual language.
- **The form is a literal postcard, rendered from the Sealed page.** The Low Sun scene that closes the round is the picture; the composed sentence is the message — prose that makes sense to a stranger, *a sentence, not a scorecard*; the roman figure is stamped where a stamp goes; and the imprint becomes the postmark (course and date, or the date alone when the round had no map). It's rendered off-screen from the static scene, never a screenshot.
- **The sun's height is the declared arc.** The sun sits lower as a thought nears being outgrown; the graduation card is the sun touching the horizon. **The app declares the arc; the golfer never composes it.** A falling count the golfer could see would be the first feature to reward under-tapping, and honest taps are the substrate of everything — the Goodhart guard.
- **The way in is a quiet lowercase link** — "send a postcard" — on the Sealed page, arriving late after the door, with **the seal as the focus moment**.
- **The graduation card has different timing by nature.** Graduation is retrospective: at the seal, the app can't know that a round was the thought's last. So the card arrives like a letter. The book writes first, on a surface the player visits — never a push notification, because *the app never calls you* — and sharing follows the player accepting the news.
- **Guard-rails.** A shared artefact must make sense to a stranger without the app (the gate that a count must interpret itself, turned outwards). A label must be earned by taps, never assembled from flattery — no Barnum statements, no onboarding personality quiz. No invite-walls: the artefact carries the pressmark and advertises itself. Shareability is a per-thought property — *Pins Are a Psyop* is a flex, *The Heckler* is confessional. And cadence respects the quiet register: no recap engine.

## Consequences

- Unscheduled — behind the release line, and [“First release at the maps-free loop”](./first-release-maps-free-loop) is untouched. The design exploration can run now; the build is a later phase.
- The sun on round postcards stays where the Sealed page has it today until there's a way to detect graduation and move it honestly. The design carries the intent; the mechanism waits.
- Per-thought shareability joins the thought's authored properties, alongside its read pack, when the layer is built.
- The postcard is the app's first outward-facing act. The Sealed page is a ceremony close, and the link mustn't break the hush — that register test is part of the phase's definition of done.

## Amendment — 21 August 2026: the stamp, and a noun the glossary bans

Two clarifications; the decision itself is unchanged and the postcard's elements are exactly as specified.

**The stamp.** The original wording, "the mark (the serif 1) is the stamp", was written on the day the pressmark took over the app icon from an earlier serif "1" figure. Read without that context, it invites the wrong inference — that the stamp changed with the icon. It didn't. The stamp is the **roman figure**, which [“The design-system specimen is the paper register’s authority”](./adopt-design-system-specimen) keeps as the die for stamping. What the figure lost was the icon, not the die. The pressmark is the icon and the masthead; on a postcard it's what the artefact carries out into the world, not the thing stamped on it.

**The noun.** The glossary allows *to mark* as a verb and forbids the noun "a mark". Two lines of the original broke it; they now say **the roman figure is stamped where a stamp goes** and **the artefact carries the pressmark and advertises itself**.

## Considered and rejected

- **A golfer-composed trend on the card** — the falling line, rendered. Rejected: it's the strongest hint of graduation, but it makes the count a target, and under-tapping to look better corrupts the substrate.
- **Invite-gated content** — the Airbuds move of a fuller recap for referrals. Rejected outright: coercion against the register, and the artefact should earn its spread.
- **A recap cadence** — weekly or seasonal cards pushed to the player. Rejected: the app never calls you; shares are sent from moments, not scheduled.
- **Sharing book pages directly.** Rejected: the book stays intimate — postcards *from* the book, never pages *of* it.
