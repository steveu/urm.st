The first drafted tap rules counted successes: "Tap when you keep the full routine." "Tap when you swing at eighty." "Tap when the grip stays soft."

A success tap needs a denominator to mean anything — out of how many shots? — and a ritual like "tap the routine" fires on every shot until it just counts shots played.

So when I reviewed the starter thoughts, I inverted seven of the drafted rules to count the lapse instead. *Full Routine* became "Tap when you catch yourself skipping it." *Swing at Eighty*, since benched pending research, became "Tap when you catch yourself going after one." A lapse is an event: it happens or it doesn't, and the count reads on its own. Silence reads as a strong stretch.

Positive taps survive only where the decision is bounded — *Pins Are a Psyop*, which taps when you take the boring target — or where the tap is itself the ritual, as in *Ancient History*, where pressing is the act of letting the last shot go.

## Consequences

- **A tap means different things per thought.** The rule grew into a small taxonomy of directions: **catch** (a lapse caught), **ritual** (the tap is the practice), **win** (a good decision taken) and **purchase** (a gamble bought, whatever the outcome). Later, some rituals became **reinforcement counts** — a press on every shot, read as a rep log. The read after the round has to be read through the thought's direction — the same clause, "none on the par threes", praises a catch thought and indicts *Pins*.
- **Totals across thoughts are meaningless.** A *Pins* win and a *Heckler* catch aren't the same currency. Reads are per thought and over time.
- **Catch thoughts graduate.** As the habit sets in, the count falls towards zero and you drop the reminder while keeping the behaviour. *Pins* is the one thought that doesn't graduate: its count rises to a plateau and holds, so a *falling* count is the warning.
- **Silence is never read alone.** A quiet round could be clean, or full of slips too small to catch. It's read against the player's own presence rating, never on its own.
- **It became a gate.** "Does the count interpret itself?" is now asked of every thought. It removed one outright: *No Heroes* tapped every hero shot bought, which measured how often you gambled, not whether you were right to ([“Remove No Heroes from the thought menu”](./remove-no-heroes)).

## Considered and rejected

- **Counting the release for *The Heckler*** ("tap every time you let one go by"). It just measured how loud your head was. Catching the thought itself risked becoming the rumination the thought opposes, so the catch moved to the visible behaviour — the mutter, the stomp, the rushed next shot — and is knowingly an under-count.
- **A catch for *Pins*** ("tap when you flag-hunt"). It would audit a poor decision once the ball is gone, which trains decision rumination.
