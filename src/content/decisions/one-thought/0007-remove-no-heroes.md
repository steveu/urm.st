*No Heroes* was the menu's risk-and-reward thought, in the Decisions family: *Some stories are worth the six. Fewer than you think.* — *Tap when the story wins.* It had been built, researched and shipped as an internal dossier the day before: 30 studies and 11 quotes verified, none dropped.

Its tap direction was **purchase**. The player taps when the hero shot wins the decision — *including when the shot comes off* — logging a story *bought*, not a sin, with whether it was worth it judged later, at the round-end read. The research pointed to purchase over a "catch" (counting only the failures) on outcome-bias grounds: a catch that fires only on the failed gamble institutionalises the very bias the thought exists to defeat. It also suggested the thought is one you never outgrow, with a distinctive success signature — a count that falls to a low non-zero floor, gated by how often the gamble was worth it.

On review, the count logic didn't hold up.

The decision: **remove No Heroes from the app's thought menu.** The curated core drops from eleven thoughts to ten. **The research is kept** as the record — the dossier stays, unlinked and unindexed.

## Why: the count can't interpret itself

A purchase tally counts *how often the hero shot won your decision* — your indulgence **rate**. There's a principled case for that. It corrects the availability bias the thought is named against: you go for it more often than you remember, and a decision is good or bad before the ball lands. But the count measures **volume, not wisdom**. Going for it five times is right for Seve and reckless for a 20-handicapper, and the raw count can't tell them apart, because whether the aggression pays depends on your conversion rate — an **outcome**. So "five stories bought" means nothing on its own.

The model's answer was a two-dimensional read: the count *and* a worth-it hit rate. But that second dimension needs to know whether it was worth it, which needs the outcome — exactly what the app deliberately does **not** capture at the tap. The app's spine is one tap with the phone in your pocket, and asking "did it come off?" at the shot re-imports the outcome bias the model exists to escape.

The intended fix — pairing taps with the score and the map *at the read* — was precisely the part still **open**: the rule for choosing which clause the composed sentence uses hadn't been settled, and No Heroes leans on it harder than any other thought. A catch thought's count interprets itself: fewer is better, full stop. No Heroes' count is close to meaningless on its own. Shipping a thought whose count can't stand without an unbuilt, bias-prone read isn't worth it. Pull it.

## Consequences

- **The Decisions family is now thin** — just *Pins Are a Psyop*, which counts wins. The risk-and-reward "hero shot" ground is unoccupied; *The Career Eight-Iron* remains the benched Decisions candidate.
- **The purchase tap direction now has no live thought.** It stays in the taxonomy — catch, ritual, win, purchase — as the slot a future risk-and-reward thought would fill. The analysis behind it stands.
- **The research is kept as the record**: a sound idea with an unsound instrument, documented honestly.
- **A design gate falls out, reusable for every remaining thought: a thought's count must interpret itself.** If it needs an outcome the app doesn't capture, or a read that isn't built, to mean anything, it isn't ready to ship.
- The polish on the on-course line and the newsletter title that prompted this review is moot.

## Considered and rejected

- **Capture shot success with each tap.** Rejected. It breaks the one-tap, phone-in-pocket spine, and "did it come off?" at the shot is the outcome-biased judgement the purchase model exists to escape — it would import the bias at the moment.
- **Switch No Heroes to a catch, counting only the hero shots that blew up.** Rejected. A catch fires only on the failed gamble and stays silent on the one that came off, institutionalising outcome bias (Baron & Hershey), and it makes the tap a self-rebuke that trains rumination over decisions (Kinrade). The research had already ruled it out, and it isn't what a player who *enjoys* the hero shot needs.

## Deferred

- **Keep it as it is and lean on the read.** Viable only once the read can lay taps over the score and the map, and that proves out. If it does, No Heroes can come back from the kept research.
